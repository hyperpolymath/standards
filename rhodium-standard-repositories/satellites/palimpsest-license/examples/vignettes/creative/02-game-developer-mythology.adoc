== Creative Vignette: The Game Developer’s Mythological Inheritance

=== Scenario: Indie Game Developer with Cultural Mythology

*Creator*: Amara Okonkwo *Work*: _Spirit Roads_ (indie puzzle-platformer
game) *Cultural Context*: Nigerian Igbo cosmology and migration
narratives *License*: Palimpsest v0.4

'''''

=== The Story

Amara Okonkwo grew up straddling two worlds. Born in Birmingham to
Nigerian parents, she spent her summers in Enugu, listening to her
grandmother’s stories of _Ani_ (the earth goddess), _Chi_ (personal
spirits), and the sacred marketplace where ancestors and living
negotiate fate. When her grandmother passed, Amara felt the weight of
those stories—how they shaped her understanding of choice, consequence,
and belonging.

She decided to create _Spirit Roads_, a puzzle-platformer where players
navigate between the physical world and the spirit realm, solving
challenges that honour Igbo philosophical concepts. The game’s central
mechanic—balancing obligations to community and personal destiny—drew
directly from the concept of _Chi_ and _Ani_ in tension.

But Amara knew this wasn’t just "`her`" game. It belonged to a lineage:
her grandmother’s oral tradition, the broader Igbo diaspora’s struggle
to maintain cultural memory, and the countless unnamed storytellers who
kept these philosophies alive through colonialism and displacement.

==== Why Palimpsest?

Amara considered Creative Commons, but it felt insufficient. CC BY-SA
would require attribution to "`Amara Okonkwo,`" but not to the Igbo
cosmology that made the game meaningful. CC BY-NC would prevent
commercial use, but she wanted the game to be commercially viable—she
just wanted that commerce to honour its cultural roots.

The Palimpsest License offered something different:

[arabic]
. *Emotional Lineage Protection*: Derivatives would have to acknowledge
the Igbo philosophical framework, not just Amara as legal author
. *Cultural Context Preservation*: Anyone modding or adapting the game
couldn’t strip the cultural meaning from its mechanics
. *AI Training Consent*: Gaming companies couldn’t train AI level
generators on her culturally-specific design patterns without permission
. *Symbolic Attribution*: Credit to her grandmother, to Igbo oral
tradition, to the diaspora community

==== Licensing the Game

Amara created comprehensive attribution metadata:

[source,markdown]
----
## Spirit Roads - Cultural and Emotional Context

**Creator**: Amara Okonkwo
**Cultural Lineage**: Igbo cosmology, specifically concepts of Chi (personal destiny) and Ani (collective earth obligation)
**Emotional Intent**: To honour my grandmother Adaeze Okonkwo and to create a space where diaspora children can see our philosophy as beautiful, complex, and game-worthy

### Symbolic Elements

- **The Dual Realms**: Represent the Igbo understanding that spiritual and physical worlds co-exist
- **The Marketplace Crossroads**: Based on *Nkwo* markets as liminal spaces where deals are struck between living and ancestors
- **The Chi Compass**: Visualizes personal destiny vs. communal obligation, a tension central to Igbo ethics

### Appropriate Uses

- Educational contexts exploring African game design
- Mods that add new levels while preserving the philosophical framework
- Academic analysis of cultural representation in gaming
- Let's Play videos that engage meaningfully with the cultural themes

### Inappropriate Uses

- Stripping the cultural context to create a "generic spirit world" game
- Using the game's design patterns in AI training for procedural generation without consent
- Commercial derivatives that profit from Igbo aesthetics while erasing Igbo philosophy
- Mods that mock, trivialize, or misrepresent Igbo spiritual concepts
----

==== The Test Case

Six months after release, a popular gaming YouTuber with 2 million
subscribers created a Let’s Play series. He loved the game, but
consistently mispronounced Igbo terms, joked about the "`weird African
stuff,`" and skipped dialogue explaining the cultural philosophy.

Amara reached out politely:

____
"`Thank you for playing _Spirit Roads_! I noticed some cultural elements
got lost in the commentary. The Palimpsest License requires preserving
cultural context—would you be willing to pin a comment with correct
pronunciations and links to Igbo philosophy resources? I’m happy to
provide them.`"
____

The YouTuber was receptive. He added a pinned comment, re-recorded his
intro with proper pronunciations, and even created a "`Cultural Guide`"
video with Amara. The collaboration brought new depth to his coverage
and educated his audience.

==== The AI Challenge

A year later, a major game studio’s AI research division scraped _Spirit
Roads_ along with thousands of indie games to train a procedural level
generation tool. They didn’t ask permission.

Amara discovered this through a data transparency report the studio was
legally required to publish in the EU. She immediately sent a
cease-and-desist citing Clause 1.2 (Non-Interpretive AI Systems require
consent):

____
“Your AI training dataset includes _Spirit Roads_, licensed under
Palimpsest v0.4. This is an Interpretive AI system (generating new game
content based on learned patterns). Training requires my explicit
written consent, which you did not obtain.

I request: 1. Immediate removal of _Spirit Roads_ from your training
dataset 2. Deletion of any model weights derived from my work 3. Public
acknowledgment of this violation 4. A commitment to respect Palimpsest
licenses in future data collection

If you wish to use _Spirit Roads_ for AI training in the future, contact
me to discuss terms that honour the cultural lineage this game
represents.”
____

The studio initially pushed back, arguing "`fair use`" for research.
Amara’s lawyer cited the Palimpsest License’s explicit prohibition and
the Hague Convention’s recognition of licensing terms. The studio, not
wanting negative publicity about exploiting African cultural heritage,
agreed to all terms.

Additionally, they offered Amara a consulting contract to help them
develop ethical AI training practices for culturally significant games.
She negotiated to bring in two other Igbo game developers, creating a
Cultural Advisory Board for the studio’s AI division.

=== Key Takeaways

==== For Creators

[arabic]
. *Cultural work needs cultural protection*: Technical licenses don’t
address cultural meaning
. *Document your lineage*: Make the cultural context explicit and
legally protected
. *Enforcement is possible*: Even indie creators can stand up to major
corporations
. *Education over litigation*: Start with friendly contact; most
violations stem from ignorance
. *Leverage violations*: Turn compliance into collaboration
opportunities

==== For Users

[arabic]
. *Engagement requires understanding*: If you’re using culturally-rooted
work, learn the context
. *Attribution means more than a name*: Credit the cultural tradition,
not just the individual
. *AI training isn’t neutral*: Procedural generation from cultural work
requires consent
. *Respect the "`inappropriate uses`" section*: Creators know what would
dishonour their work

==== For Platforms

[arabic]
. *License metadata matters*: Gaming platforms should display cultural
context prominently
. *Modding policies need nuance*: Allow mods that respect cultural
frameworks, restrict those that don’t
. *AI scraping requires consent*: Don’t assume indie games are "`open
data`" for training
. *Support cultural creators*: Provide tools for enforcing attribution
and context preservation

=== Legal Analysis

==== Palimpsest Clauses in Action

*Clause 1.2 (AI Training Consent)*: The studio’s procedural generation
tool was clearly Interpretive AI, creating new game content by learning
patterns. This required explicit consent, which wasn’t obtained.

*Clause 2.3 (Metadata Preservation)*: The YouTuber’s initial coverage
didn’t violate metadata stripping (he didn’t redistribute the game), but
his commentary failed to preserve cultural context, which Amara
successfully addressed through education rather than litigation.

*Emotional Lineage Protection*: The license’s focus on "`emotional
fidelity`" allowed Amara to negotiate not just legal compliance, but
meaningful cultural engagement from both the YouTuber and the studio.

==== Why This Worked

[arabic]
. *Clear documentation*: Amara’s cultural context file made her intent
legally explicit
. *Proportional response*: She tried education first, litigation second
. *Jurisdictional clarity*: The Palimpsest License’s use of Dutch law
gave her a strong legal foundation
. *Community impact*: Framing the issue as cultural heritage protection
resonated publicly

=== Cultural Sensitivity Notes

This vignette illustrates:

* *Diaspora cultural stewardship*: Amara’s right to draw from Igbo
tradition while acknowledging she’s one voice in a larger community
* *Respectful commercialization*: Making money from cultural work isn’t
exploitation if the cultural meaning is preserved and honoured
* *Education as enforcement*: Not every violation needs a lawsuit;
sometimes people just need to understand why context matters
* *Collaborative remediation*: Turning a violation into a partnership
benefits everyone
