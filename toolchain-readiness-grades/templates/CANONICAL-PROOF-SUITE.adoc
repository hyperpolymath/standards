= TRG Canonical Proof Suite — Mathematical, Scientific, Engineering (M/S/E-CPS)
:status: v1.0 — gate to leave grade E
:date: 2026-04-12
:toc: preamble

This is the *Canonical Proof Suite* referenced from
`TOOLCHAIN-READINESS-GRADES.adoc` §6.6. A hyperpolymath language
toolchain MUST be able to *express, machine-check, and re-run nightly*
every entry in this suite to qualify above grade *E*.

The suite is versioned. Suite expansions over time are expected and
may demote A-grade components — that demotion is acceptable and is the
mechanism by which TRG keeps pace with the frontier.

== Pass condition

A toolchain passes the M/S/E-CPS iff:

. Every entry in this document has at least one machine-checked proof
  in the language under audit.
. Every proof is re-checked nightly under at least one qualifying
  prover (see `templates/QUALIFYING-PROVERS.adoc`).
. Every proof contains zero `believe_me`, `assert_total`, `Admitted`,
  `sorry`, `postulate`, `unsafeCoerce`, or any other escape hatch
  from the banned-construct list in `TOOLCHAIN-READINESS-GRADES.adoc` §6.5.
. The corpus of proofs is publicly archived and reproducibly buildable
  from a clean checkout in a fresh environment.

== How a language toolchain ports this suite

. *Identify a qualifying prover* from `templates/QUALIFYING-PROVERS.adoc` (Idris2,
  Lean 4, Rocq, or Agda for v1).
. *For each suite entry* — find an existing proof in that prover's
  ecosystem (the third column above is a starter map). Port it to the
  language under audit's proof system *if* the language is itself a
  proof system; otherwise, use the language to *encode the statement
  and the proof obligation*, then discharge the obligation in the
  qualifying prover and link the artefact.
. *Wire each proof into nightly CI*. Every proof must be re-checked
  every 24 hours; any proof failure for more than 24 hours triggers
  immediate demotion per `TOOLCHAIN-READINESS-GRADES.adoc` §5.8.
. *Archive the proof corpus* under `.machine_readable/proofs/canonical-proof-suite/`
  in the language repo. Each proof gets a JSON sidecar with: prover
  used, proof file path, last-checked timestamp, kernel version,
  elapsed time, and the soundness-proof citation for the prover
  itself.
. *Publish a status table* in the language repo's README showing
  pass/fail for every suite entry. The table is itself a claim and
  is itself subject to the invariant-path doc-claims grounder.

== Versioning

* *v1.0* (2026-04-12) — initial set of 15 entries (5 mathematics + 5
  science + 5 engineering). Suite SHALL be reviewed and may be
  expanded annually.
* When the suite is expanded, every existing A-grade component is
  re-evaluated against the new suite at the next assessment cycle.
  Components that no longer pass are demoted to grade B until they
  add the new entries.
* Suite expansions are co-signed by at least two reviewers (per the
  dual-use co-signing rule) and announced 90 days before they take
  effect, to give A-grade components a fair window to add the new
  entries.

== Notes on choice of entries

The 15 entries in v1.0 are deliberately *representative* not
*exhaustive*. The criterion for inclusion is:

. *Educational standing* — every working scientist or engineer has
  encountered the result and can recognise it.
. *Existing formal proofs* — at least one of the qualifying provers
  already has a machine-checked proof somewhere in its ecosystem,
  so the porting work is bounded.
. *Compositional independence* — no two entries reduce trivially to
  each other, so all 15 must be independently exercised.
. *Domain coverage* — five entries from each of mathematics, science,
  and engineering, so no domain dominates and a toolchain that can
  only express one kind of formal reasoning is excluded.

== Open invitation

The suite is open to challenge. If you think an entry is the wrong
choice, propose a replacement with a justification. If you think the
domain coverage is unbalanced, propose a rebalancing with evidence.
The suite earns trust by surviving critique, not by avoiding it.
