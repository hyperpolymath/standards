[.lead]
This document does four things. It inventories what the commercial
code-quality and PR-review tools sell, and sorts every feature into
*symbolic* (a rule, a grammar, a dataflow, a metric) or *neural* (needs a
learned model). It reads the estate's own quality machinery — Hypatia, the
gitbot-fleet, panic-attack, pons-asinorum, VeriSimDB, cicd-squabbler,
cicd-suite, feedback-o-tron, ECHIDNA, the proof and type repositories, the
scaffolding and container tooling — and says, with file-level evidence, which
rung of Kautz's neurosymbolic ladder each of them actually stands on today.
It names what is missing between here and the top rung. And it lays out a
multi-year programme in waves, the first of which is cheap: take back the
formulaic checks the paid bots are eating credit on, route them through the
estate's own symbolic layer, and leave the paid neural reviewers only the
slice that genuinely needs a model.

Every claim about an estate repository cites a repo-relative path. Every
claim about a vendor cites a URL in <<sources>>. Figures that could not be
verified from inside this session are marked as such. Where a number is a
vendor's own claim it says so.

== Verdict in one page

*Where we are.* The estate is a *Symbolic[Neuro]* machine (Kautz type 2) in
design and a *purely symbolic* machine in operation. Hypatia's 35 Elixir rule
modules own the loop; its eight neural networks are consulted only to
_demote_ confidence or route novel findings to humans, and they are trained on
an outcome log that is 99% synthetic successes (`hypatia/.claude/CLAUDE.md`
Known Gaps 7; `hypatia/docs/architecture/NEURAL-ARCHITECTURE.adoc`). The one
actuator that could close the learning loop — the gitbot-fleet — has every
mutation path interlocked with `exit 78`
(`gitbot-fleet/docs/AUTOMATION-QUARANTINE.adoc`). The only neural components
that _act_ on estate PRs today are CodeRabbit and Codex, bolted on from
outside at Kautz type 1, unconnected to any estate database, and metered:
CodeRabbit's Advanced plan grants one included review per hour and reports
"0 remain" on an ordinary standards PR
(hyperpolymath/standards#1295 review thread). So the neural pools we own sit
unused and the neural reviews we rent are rationed.

*What we lack, ranked.*

. *A truthful outcome signal.* Fleet "success" is recorded when a fix script
  exits 0, without a rescan (`gitbot-fleet/audits/fleet-wiring-2026-09-26.md`
  §1, §7). Nothing learned from that log is real. Everything above type 2
  depends on fixing this first.
. *A reachable records store.* The live store is a git repository of flat
  files; `verisim-api` ships in-memory, with auth hard-wired off, cross-request
  transactions returning 501 and the Groove/feedback router never mounted
  (`verisimdb/rust-core/verisim-api/src/{auth.rs,lib.rs,groove.rs}`,
  `KNOWN-ISSUES.adoc` #29, #30). The standards-side Hypatia rules in
  `hypatia-rules/` are written against a VeriSimDB instance on port 8097 that
  does not exist.
. *One findings bus.* SARIF is emitted by Hypatia, panic-attack, pons,
  oikosbot, cipherbot, seambot and accessibilitybot, and ingested by nobody
  in the estate. cicd-suite's 30 bash gates emit nothing structured at all.
  The verdict grid (`docs/VERDICT-GRID-SPEC.adoc`) is the right surface; it
  has three permanent rows and no fleet report-backs feeding it yet.
. *Stable rule identity.* panic-attack's SARIF exporter and its aggregate
  module disagree on which PA number each category carries
  (`panic-attack/src/report/sarif.rs:118-147` vs
  `src/aggregate/mod.rs:816-835`). A learning system cannot learn against a
  moving key.
. *The symbolic "floor-raising" layer.* The repository-hygiene, prose, link,
  template and asset rules that would replace most of what CodeRabbit
  charges for exist in pons only as 18 fixture corpora under
  `pons-asinorum/deferred/rookie-fixtures/`; `main` implements 10 code-smell
  rules in 5 languages.
. *A model anywhere that is ours.* ECHIDNA's Julia ranker has no trained
  weights (its roadmap Stage 2); feedback-o-tron's "synthesis" is regex and
  YAML; oikosbot's Datalog policy engine "has never executed"
  (`oikosbot/DEBT.adoc`). The estate has no learned component with a
  measured lift.

*Where we are going.* Kautz's sixth category, Neuro[Symbolic], is unclaimed
by anyone in the literature (Sarker et al. 2021 found zero papers; van
Bekkum et al. 2021 decline to interpret it). The honest target for this
programme is not to be the first. It is to run a *type 2 machine with real
learned proposers*, add *type 3 learned front-ends* that emit symbolic IR
(Trope IR, taint specs, kanren facts) for exact reasoners, use provers and
executors as *type 4 data generators* for those proposers, and keep the
"distil to dumb rules" arrow that is Hypatia's founding idea as a measured,
falsifiable loop. Section <<ladder>> defines six estate rungs, K0–K6, each
with a falsifier, so "what level are we at" stops being a matter of opinion.

*What to do first (Wave 0 in weeks; Wave 1 across two quarters under the pons-native ruling).* Make outcomes
true; deploy VeriSimDB with auth and persistence; mount Groove; fix the
PatternQuery bug that makes ingested scans invisible; freeze PA identifiers;
build the formulaic-check eater on top of pons and a SARIF adapter so that
docstring coverage, container-file lint, Guix manifests, link rot, template
placeholders, formatting, commit-message shape and pre-merge conventions are
all estate checks reporting into the verdict grid; then pin CodeRabbit and
Codex to the neural slice only (summaries, intent-vs-implementation,
naming) by path and event, and measure the credit saved.

== What the market sells, and what is formulaic

=== The feature inventory, classified

The full per-tool inventory with sources is in <<appendix-tools>>. The
pattern across the market is uniform enough to state once:

* *The symbolic half is commodity.* Every vendor's rule engine, taint
  analysis, duplication, complexity, coverage arithmetic, secret regexes,
  dependency pinning, IaC lint and container lint is either an OSS linter
  under the hood or a proprietary reimplementation of one. CodeRabbit
  bundles 59 OSS tools (ruff, biome, eslint, clippy, shellcheck, hadolint,
  yamllint, actionlint, markdownlint, semgrep, gitleaks, checkov, trivy,
  osv-scanner, …) and its "pre-merge docstring coverage check" is a function
  count. Codacy wraps ESLint, Semgrep, PMD, RuboCop, ShellCheck, Hadolint,
  Checkov and Trivy. Codeac is a pure aggregator. Codecov does no analysis of
  its own: it ingests the coverage and JUnit files your toolchain already
  produces and sells the diff arithmetic and the comment. Qlty, Trunk and
  MegaLinter are explicitly orchestrators of 70–100+ linters; MegaLinter is
  AGPL and free.
* *The exceptions are Sonar and DeepSource*, whose analysers (Sonar's
  interprocedural taint engine and cognitive-complexity metric in
  particular) are genuinely in-house. Semgrep's paid moat is interfile taint,
  curated rules, reachability SCA and the LLM "Assistant".
* *The neural slice is narrow and is also where the complaints are.*
  Genuinely model-dependent features: PR walkthroughs and sequence-diagram
  extraction; intent-vs-implementation mismatch against the title or linked
  issue; naming and readability judgements; cross-file architectural
  suggestions; natural-language rule authoring; learned triage of false
  positives (Semgrep Assistant and Memories, Codacy Smart FP Triage,
  CodeRabbit learnings); semantic autofix and test generation. The sentiment
  data concentrates on that slice: CodeRabbit "40+ inline comments on large
  refactors, most noise", throttling "to a few reviews per hour" even on paid
  tiers; Greptile "pretty much pure noise"; Copilot code review at ~35%
  precision in third-party benchmarks; Semgrep's 60%-auto-triage figure is
  vendor-reported and an AppSec analyst warns suppression must be measured
  separately from recall.
* *Pricing follows the neural slice.* Seats, credits, "premium requests",
  per-run billing (Bugbot moved to ~$1–1.50 per run in 2026), hourly review
  caps. Lines-of-code pricing (Sonar) is the other recurring gripe.

== The estate as a Kautz machine

=== The ladder, as Kautz drew it

Kautz's six categories (AAAI-20 Engelmore lecture; _AI Magazine_ 43(1), 2022)
are, in his order:

. *symbolic Neuro symbolic* — symbols in, symbols out, all processing
  neural. Every LLM, including CodeRabbit and Codex, by construction.
. *Symbolic[Neuro]* — a symbolic solver owns the loop and calls a network as
  a subroutine. AlphaGo; AlphaProof; "most autonomous vehicles".
. *Neuro | Symbolic* — a neural front-end turns raw input into symbols that
  a separate exact reasoner then processes.
. *Neuro: Symbolic → Neuro* — symbolic knowledge generates training data; the
  rules are compiled away at training time.
. *Neuro_Symbolic* — logic compiled into the structure or loss of the
  network (Logic Tensor Networks, DeepProbLog, neural theorem provers).
. *Neuro[Symbolic]* — the "holy grail": a neural engine that contains an
  exact reasoner and learns when to invoke it (Kahneman's System 1 calling
  System 2). No accepted example exists.

The 2025–26 code-analysis literature keeps the symbolic engine as the
authority and uses the model either to *write the symbolic program* (IRIS
infers taint specs for CodeQL; QLCoder and ARQ synthesise CodeQL queries;
Lemur proposes loop invariants and an SMT solver checks them; AutoVerus and
APOLLO write Verus and Lean proofs that the kernel checks) or to *triage its
output* (BugLens, Semgrep Assistant). None of them is type 6. The strongest
are type 2 with a learned proposer — which is the shape Hypatia, panic-attack
and ECHIDNA already have on paper. The literature is on our side; the gap is
operational, not architectural.

== The estate ladder: K0–K6 with falsifiers

Kautz's categories classify architectures. To run a programme we need rungs
that classify *capabilities*, each with a test that would show we are not
there. These are estate-local definitions; they map to Kautz's types but add
the operational conditions his taxonomy leaves implicit.

[cols="1,2,4,4",options="header"]
|===
|Rung |Kautz |Capability |Falsifier (the test that says "not yet")

|K0
|—
|Symbolic checks, enforced. Every reporter emits SARIF; findings carry
HYP-01 `support`/`evidence`/`level`; the verdict grid is the one PR surface;
a check that cannot fail is caught (oikosbot `infallible_gate_candidates`).
|A PR merges with a red grid row; a reporter has zero lifetime failures and
no `grey` reason; two reporters disagree on a rule ID for one finding.

|K1
|1
|Neural proposers outside the loop, _measured_. CodeRabbit/Codex comments
are classified per PR as reproducible-by-K0 vs genuinely neural, and the
cost per genuinely-neural comment is tracked.
|No month-over-month figure for "credit spent on comments a K0 rule could
have made".

|K2
|2
|Symbolic[Neuro] with real data. Hypatia's pools train on rescan-verified
outcomes; neural demotion changes a dispatch tier at least once a week with
a recorded reason; `ProverRecommender` MRR beats the 0.66 baseline on
held-out `proof_attempts`.
|Replay last quarter's outcomes with and without `enhance_with_neural/1`:
if dispatch precision is unchanged (±1%) the pools are inert.

|K3
|3
|Learned front-ends emit symbolic IR for exact reasoners. A model proposes
taint source/sink/sanitiser specs that kanren consumes (IRIS pattern);
proposes `@covers` annotations that `aggregate/` checks; lowers a function
to Trope IR that trope-checker verdicts.
|Every proposal is accepted (the exact reasoner is not refusing anything) or
every proposal is rejected (the front-end is noise).

|K4
|4 and its inverse
|Provers and executors generate training data (pons falsifier corpora grown
by executor-verified self-play; ECHIDNA `proof_attempts`), and learned
patterns are distilled back into `lib/rules/` modules with a falsifier
corpus each ("dumb rules from smart learning" executed).
|No rule in `lib/rules/` can point at the outcome rows it was distilled from.

|K5
|5
|Logic in the model. k9 invariants and MUST contractiles become
differentiable constraints on the confidence estimators (LTN-style), so a
prediction that violates an invariant is penalised in training, not filtered
after.
|Removing the constraint term changes nothing measurable.

|K6
|6
|A learned invocation policy for exact reasoning, inside one trained
system: a small estate-trained model (TRM/HRM-class, trained on estate
outcomes) decides, per finding, whether to answer from pattern, call kanren,
call ECHIDNA, or hold for a human — and the decision policy is learned, not
scripted.
|The policy, ablated to a fixed rule, performs as well. (If it does, we are
at K2 with extra steps, and should say so.)
|===

Honest position: the estate is at *K0 with holes* (SARIF not ingested, IDs
unstable, fake gates present) and has *K1 unmeasured*. K2 is one quarter
away once outcomes are true and VeriSimDB is reachable. K3 and K4 are
where the proof and type repositories pay off. K5 is research with a clear
recipe. K6 is research without one; the plan reaches for it only after K4
has produced data to learn a policy from.

=== Wave 6 — new technology, placed honestly

* *NCM.* The acronym most plausibly refers to *Next-Concept Modeling*
  (Stanford Hazy Research / Together AI, arXiv:2610.14162, 12 October 2026):
  a two-stage generation scheme — a multiscale residual VQ-VAE tokenises text
  into coarse-to-fine "concept" codes, and a transformer generates one level
  at a time, all codes in a level in parallel. It is post-autoregressive,
  not post-transformer, and its claims (10–31× faster than masked-diffusion
  LMs at matched quality, 4–5× fewer training tokens) are self-reported
  with no replication yet. Its relevance here is narrow: faster _generation_
  of prose (summaries, draft issues) if the estate ever hosts its own
  generator. It does not change the symbolic plan. (If "NCM" meant
  something else, the second candidate is "Neural Code Models", the SE
  research term for any neural model over code, which is not a new
  architecture.)
* *Small reasoning models.* The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (27M
  parameters) and the Tiny Recursive Model (7M) learn puzzle-class reasoning
  from ~1k examples. They are the right size for a K6 _invocation policy_
  trained on estate outcomes, and small enough to run on a CI runner. They
  are not code reviewers.
* *Executor-verified self-play.* The Absolute Zero Reasoner paradigm (one
  model proposes and solves its own tasks, rewarded by an executor) is a
  K4 data generator for pons: propose candidate falsifier programs for a
  rule, keep the ones the rule and the executor disagree on. No external
  data, no vendor.
* *LLM proposes, solver checks.* Lemur/LLMDFA/IRIS-style loops are the K3
  recipe: a rented model proposes taint specs or invariants; kanren,
  ECHIDNA or Z3 checks; only checked results enter the store. The rented
  model never touches the verdict.
* *Diffusion and SSM code models* (Mercury, Gemini Diffusion, Mamba-3,
  Titans) matter only if the estate hosts a generator; defer.
